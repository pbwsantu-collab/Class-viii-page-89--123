Lesson 9: Summer Friends

By Mary Lamb

Let's start:

Mary Ann Lamb (1764 -1847) was a famous English poet and writer. She is best known today for her collaboration with her brother Charles Lamb on the collection Tales from Shakespeare. She and her brother presided over a literary circle in London that included poets like Wordsworth and Coleridge.

2. Which of the birds mentioned above appears to be the closest friend to mankind? Why do you think so?

Answer: Among the birds mentioned above, the pigeon appears to be the closest friend to mankind. Historically, pigeons have lived in close proximity to human settlements for centuries. They faithfully served humanity as reliable messengers, carrying vital letters across vast distances before modern communication existed. Their gentle nature, trusting behavior, and ability to form strong bonds with people make them a symbol of peace and companionship, showing true loyalty to human beings.

3. How would you feel if you are separated from your friend?

Answer: If I am separated from my friend, I would feel an immense sense of sadness and loneliness. True friends bring joy, comfort, and emotional support to our daily lives. Their absence creates an empty void that is difficult to fill. I would deeply miss our shared conversations, laughter, and mutual encouragement. However, I would cherish our fond memories and try to stay connected through messages, hoping heartfeltly for a joyful reunion in the near future.

Let's read:

The Swallow is a summer bird;
He in our chimneys, when the weather
Is fine and warm, may then be heard
Chirping his notes for weeks together.
    Come there but one cold wintry day,
    Away will fly our guest the Swallow:
    And much like him we find the way
    Which many a gay young friend will follow.
In dreary days of snow and frost
Closer to Man will cling the Sparrow:
Old friends, although in life we're crost,
Their hearts to us will never narrow.
    Give me the bird—give me the friend—
    Will sing in frost—will love in sorrow—
    Whate'er mischance to-day may send,
    Will greet me with his sight to-morrow.

Word Nest:

  • gay : happy
  • dreary : dull
  • cling : to remain emotionally attached
  • crost : the poetical form of the word 'crossed'. Here it means separated
  • mischance : misfortune

Activity 3

Why do you think the swallow leaves the poet during the winter season?

Answer: The swallow leaves the poet during winter because it is a fair-weather bird that cannot endure harsh cold, frost, and scarcity of food. Symbolically, it represents selfish, fair-weather friends who stay only during happy, prosperous times. When difficulties or sorrows arrive, such companions abandon us just as the swallow flies away to warmer places. It lacks the loyalty required to stand by someone during adversity and hardship.

Activity 5

Answer the following questions:

(a) What does the swallow do in summer?

Answer: In summer, the swallow builds its nest in human chimneys and delightfully chirps its melodious notes for weeks together. It thrives in the warm, pleasant weather, enjoying the comforts of bright days. It remains a cheerful companion as long as the environment is favorable. However, its presence is entirely dependent on good weather, making it a transient guest that disappears when the warmth fades.

(b) How is the behaviour of a "gay young friend" similar to that of the swallow?

Answer: The behaviour of a "gay young friend" closely mirrors that of the swallow because both are fair-weather companions. A gay young friend stays close during times of joy, prosperity, and happiness, much like the swallow that appears only in warm summer. However, when trouble, hardship, or poverty strikes, such friends quickly leave and disappear, just as the swallow flies away at the first sign of wintry cold.

(c) What type of a friend does the poet want?

Answer: The poet wants a loyal, devoted, and steadfast friend who remains faithful through all seasons of life. She desires a companion who will offer love, comfort, and solace during times of sorrow, misfortune, and hardship, just like the sparrow that clings closer during dreary winter. The poet values a friend whose heart will never narrow or change, regardless of life's unpredictable misfortunes.

(d) Whom does the poet like—the swallow or the sparrow? Give reasons for your answer.

Answer: The poet clearly likes the sparrow. The reason is that the sparrow is a symbol of unwavering loyalty and true friendship. Unlike the swallow, which flies away when cold weather arrives, the sparrow clings closer to humankind during dreary days of snow and frost. It stands by man in difficulties, representing a true friend who sings in frost and loves in sorrow.

Activity 6(b)

Change the following sentences into indirect speech:

(i) The sailor said to us, "There will be a storm."
Answer: The sailor told us that there would be a storm.

(ii) The teacher said to the student, "Did you come to school yesterday?"
Answer: The teacher asked the student if he/she had gone to school the previous day.

(iii) The tired guest said to the host, "Please give me a glass of cold water."
Answer: The tired guest requested the host to give him/her a glass of cold water.

(iv) Rita said, "How happy I am!"
Answer: Rita exclaimed with joy that she was very happy.

(v) My grandfather said, "May you live long!"
Answer: My grandfather wished that I might live long.

The Farmer and the Ungrateful Snake

On a freezing winter morning, a kind farmer found a snake lying almost dead with cold in his field. Taking pity on it, he brought it home and warmed it near the fireplace. Soon, the snake regained its strength and vitality. However, true to its vicious nature, it raised its hood and was about to bite the farmer's innocent young son. Seeing this, the furious farmer immediately grabbed a stick and chased the dangerous snake away from his house.

Moral: Nature never changes; never expect gratitude from the wicked.

A Day With My Talking Bird

During my summer vacation, I spent a delightful day with my pet talking parrot, Mitu. In the morning, he cheerfully woke me up by calling my name loudly. We ate fresh guavas together, and he amused me by repeating funny words. In the afternoon, we played peek-a-boo inside my room, and he gently perched on my shoulder. As evening approached, he sang sweet whistles while I read books. His loving, playful presence filled my entire vacation day with immense joy, laughter, and unforgettable happiness.
